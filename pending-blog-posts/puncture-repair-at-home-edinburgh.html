Puncture Repair at Home in Edinburgh: What Can Be Fixed and How It Works

Puncture repair at your home or work in Edinburgh & West Lothian. When a tyre can be repaired, when it can't, and what happens when we arrive. 24/7.

A slow puncture rarely happens at a good time. The good news is that you usually do not need to drive to a garage on a soft tyre, or wait in a queue: a mobile tyre fitter can come to your driveway, your workplace car park or the roadside and repair the tyre there — if it is safe to repair. Here is how to tell, and what to expect.

Can my tyre be repaired?

UK tyre repairs follow the British Standard BS AU 159[1]. For a normal car tyre, a repair is usually possible when:

  • The puncture is in the central part of the tread — roughly the middle three-quarters of the tread width. Damage nearer the edge (the shoulder) or in the sidewall cannot be safely repaired, because that area flexes the most and carries much of the load[1].
  • The hole is small — no more than 6 mm across for a car tyre (a typical screw or nail)[1].
  • The tyre has not been driven on while flat. Running on a flat tyre damages it from the inside, even when the outside looks fine.
  • There is no other damage, such as cuts, bulges or earlier repairs too close by.

Run-flat tyres are a special case: many manufacturers do not allow them to be repaired, because damage inside can be hidden by the stiff sidewall[1]. We will tell you on the spot what applies to your tyre.

What happens when we arrive

  1. We check where the damage is and what caused it — nail, screw, cut or valve.
  2. We take the tyre off the wheel. A safe repair needs the tyre removed and inspected from the inside; a plug pushed in from outside is not a proper repair[1].
  3. If it can be repaired, we repair it from the inside, refit and balance the wheel, and set the pressure.
  4. If it cannot be repaired, we explain why and fit a replacement tyre. Tell us your tyre size when you call (it is printed on the sidewall, e.g. 205/55 R16) and we will bring a replacement with us, just in case.

You do not need to be at the car the whole time — just let us know where it is parked and how to reach you.

What to do until we get there

  • If the tyre is losing air quickly, do not keep driving on it. Stop somewhere safe.
  • Do not try to drive to a garage on a flat — it often turns a repairable tyre into one that has to be replaced.
  • Leave the nail or screw in the tyre; pulling it out can make the leak faster.
  • If you have a tyre sealant kit, ask us before using it: some sealants make a proper repair harder.
