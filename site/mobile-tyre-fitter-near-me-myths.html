Mobile Tyre Fitting Myths, Answered

Common myths about mobile tyre fitting — cost, quality, speed and more — addressed factually, without the hype.

Mobile tyre fitting has been around long enough that most of the myths about it are out of date. Here's a straightforward look at some of the common ones.

Myth: it costs more than a garage

It isn't a given either way: what you pay depends on the tyre, the vehicle and the job, so it's sensible to ask any provider for a price before you book. Where a fitting can safely be completed at the vehicle, a mobile appointment may also save the journey to a garage.

Myth: mobile fitters use lower-quality tyres

There's no inherent reason a tyre fitted at the roadside is any different from one fitted on a garage forecourt. What matters is the tyre that's supplied, so it's worth asking which one it is before you book.

Myth: it's slower than a garage visit

How long a fitting takes depends on the job and the vehicle. What mobile fitting removes is the time spent travelling to and from a garage.

Myth: it's only for emergencies

Emergency callouts are one use of mobile fitting, but it also suits routine replacements, seasonal tyre changes or scheduled maintenance, simply because it saves the trip to a garage.

Myth: a lost locking wheel nut key means a tow

Specialist extraction tools exist for exactly this situation. See our guide on lost locking wheel nut keys for how it's actually handled.

Myth: DIY repair is just as good

A roadside sealant or plug kit can be a temporary get-you-home measure, but it isn't a substitute for a proper repair — which needs the tyre off the wheel and inspected. See our guide on what a proper puncture repair actually involves.

Myth: mobile fitters aren't as qualified

Mobile fitting requires the same competence as garage-based fitting — balancing, correct torque, and an honest assessment of what a tyre actually needs. The setting doesn't change the skill required.

Myth: it doesn't work in bad weather

Bad weather can affect whether a job can be done safely outdoors, and a technician will always prioritise safety over pushing ahead with a job in genuinely unsafe conditions — so it's worth asking about it when you book.
